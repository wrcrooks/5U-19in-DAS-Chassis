# 5U 19-inch DAS Chassis

A 5U rack-mount direct-attached storage chassis for 48 3.5-inch drives, designed to be cooled by quiet
120 mm fans. The sheet-metal parts are laid out for laser cutting and bending by
[SendCutSend](https://sendcutsend.com/) in 0.080 in (2.032 mm) 5052-H32 aluminium; the handles and
backplane trays are 3D printed.

**Status: design in progress. Nothing here has been manufactured or thermally tested yet.** See
[Open items](#open-items) before ordering parts.

## Design summary

| | |
|---|---|
| Drives | 48 x 3.5 in, in 16 stacks of three, broad faces parallel to the airflow |
| Backplanes | 4 x Supermicro BPN-SAS3-826EL1 / BPN-SAS3-826EL1-N4 (same hole pattern), on printed trays at 105.4 mm pitch |
| Fans | 3 x 120 mm in the Bezel and 3 x 120 mm in the fan wall behind the drives |
| Envelope | 430 mm wide (436 mm over the cover flanges), 738 mm deep, 221 mm (5U) at the Bezel |
| Airflow | Front to back: Bezel, drive array, fan wall, rear electronics, Back Panel exhaust |
| Covers | Two drop-in lids (drive bay and rear electronics), no fasteners, finger holes for lifting |

The backplanes are spread apart rather than mounted edge to edge. That leaves 25 mm of air between drive
stacks, which keeps the pressure drop through the array low enough for quiet fans.

## Repository layout

```
CAD/
  Assembly - *.step        Original Onshape export, one part per file (reference, not edited)
  Complete-Assembly/       Original Onshape assembly
  Backplane Tray/          Printed backplane tray parts (original)
  Revised/                 Manufacturable parts and assembly, generated by tools/rebuild_parts.py
  Flat-Patterns/           DXF flat patterns, bend-data.json and Bend-Data.md
tools/
  sheetmetal.py            Unfolding, re-thickening and DXF writing
  rebuild_parts.py         Builds CAD/Revised/ from the Onshape export
  unfold_sheet_metal.py    Builds CAD/Flat-Patterns/ from CAD/Revised/
```

[`CAD/Flat-Patterns/Bend-Data.md`](CAD/Flat-Patterns/Bend-Data.md) is the main design document. It lists
every change from the Onshape export and the reason for it, the material and bend parameters, a bend
table per part, and the checks against SendCutSend's design rules.

## Regenerating the outputs

Requires [FreeCAD](https://www.freecad.org/) 0.20 or later (developed against 0.20.2). No other
dependencies. From the repository root:

```sh
freecadcmd tools/rebuild_parts.py       # CAD/*.step -> CAD/Revised/
freecadcmd tools/unfold_sheet_metal.py  # CAD/Revised/ -> CAD/Flat-Patterns/
```

Run both, in that order, after any change. Dimensions of the new and modified parts are constants at
the top of `tools/rebuild_parts.py`; material and bend parameters are in `MATERIAL` in
`tools/sheetmetal.py`. Both scripts check their own results (valid solids, closed contours, cut-out
counts, interference between parts) and print the findings.

## DXF conventions

Millimetres at 1:1, everything on layer `0`. Cut geometry is continuous; bend centrelines are `DASHED`.
Each pattern is drawn looking at the face the flanges fold toward, so every bend is 90° up.

## Open items

- **Thermal performance is estimated by hand, not simulated or measured.** Test one fully populated
  backplane with the intended fans before committing to the full build.
- **Fan-wall cable cut-outs** are larger than the cables need and let air recirculate. They need to be
  sized to the real cable bundles or closed with brush strip or foam.
- **Drive position on the backplane** is taken from the tray model, not measured on a real board. Check
  the Drive Support Panel openings against a populated backplane before cutting.
- **Empty drive positions** need printed blanks, or air will bypass the populated ones.
- **Dust:** fit screw-on filters to the Bezel fan positions. The Bezel is aluminium, so magnetic filters
  will not hold.
- **Covers are held by gravity only** and need securing for transport.
- The Back Panel has one 1.35 mm strip between cut-outs, carried over from the original model.
